METHOD REFERENCES

Cai, Y., Xu, W., & Zhang, F. (2021).
ikd-Tree: An Incremental K-D Tree for Robotic Applications. arXiv:2102.10808.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10808
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.10808
https://arxiv.org/html/2102.10808v1#S3.SS2
The abstract and PDF are the same source. Section III-B / Algorithm 2 describes
sorting and median-based construction with a maximal-covariance axis and extra
incremental metadata. Our static 2-D tree alternates X/Y axes and implements
neither the covariance choice nor incremental insertion/deletion/rebalancing.

Li, L., Losser, T., Yorke, C., & Piltner, R. (2014).
Fast inverse distance weighting-based spatiotemporal interpolation: A web-based
application of interpolating daily fine particulate matter PM2.5 in the
contiguous U.S. using parallel programming and k-d tree.
International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 11(9),
9101-9141. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph110909101
https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/11/9/9101
Existing proposal source; relevant full-text sections checked. Section 2.2.1
supports inverse-distance weights; Sections 2.4.3 and 2.4.5-2.4.6 describe cyclic
axis partitioning and bounded nearest-neighbor selection. The paper includes
time as a dimension; our current tree selects a date first and uses only x/y.
Its reported performance and pollution-validation results are not our results.

Original teammate link. Public max-heap functions were added in Python 3.14:
https://docs.python.org/3.14/library/heapq.html
The compatible implementation uses min-heap heappush/heapreplace with negated
priorities, as supported by the earlier API:
https://docs.python.org/3.13/library/heapq.html

Supports the Axis enum, not an interpolation method.

SPATIAL COORDINATE PREPARATION

https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/utm.html
Explains UTM as a geodetic-to-projected transformation. Real stations and query
points must share a suitable projected CRS with metre units. No coordinates
are supplied or inferred by this reference; the current CSV has none.
